Student Resource Center

Textbooks & course supplies

We know not every student can afford to buy books brand new, and you shouldn’t have to. Start with the free and low-cost options below before you pay full price.

Before you buy anything: get the exact title, edition, and ISBN from your syllabus, and ask your professor whether an older edition works and whether the book is truly required. Some listed books are barely used, so waiting until the first week of class can save you real money.

Smart moves

Seven ways to spend less on books

Ask the professor first

Ask if the book is required, whether an older edition is fine, and whether a copy is on reserve in the library.

Use the ISBN

Search by ISBN, not title, so you compare the exact edition. Note any online access code your course requires, because used books may not include one.

Borrow before you buy

Try the library, a classmate, or a student who took the class last term before you spend anything.

Check for digital options

A digital edition can cost less than print. Make sure it works for your course and check how long you keep access.

Sell or return when you’re done

Sell back books you won’t keep, and know your rental return date so late fees don’t eat your savings.

Ask your financial aid office

Some schools offer book vouchers, bookstore credit, or emergency aid. It costs nothing to ask what your school has.

Claim the tax credit

Books, supplies, and equipment you need for a course can count as qualified education expenses for the American Opportunity Credit, even if bought off campus. Rules and limits apply. See the IRS guide →
