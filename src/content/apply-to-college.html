How to actually apply to college

There's no single "apply to any college" button — but there are only a handful of real systems behind almost every application. Here's what they are, and the step-by-step process for using them.

We don't submit applications for you. Every link below goes straight to the real system or the school's own site — we don't run any of these, and there's no fee to use this page.
Where you actually apply

The real systems behind most applications

Most four-year colleges accept one (or more) of these. Check each school's own admissions page to see which they take — many accept several.

Common App

One application, submitted to 1,200+ member colleges across the U.S., public and private. The most widely used system for first-year applicants.

Visit Common App →

Coalition for College

Apply Coalition with Scoir lets you submit applications to participating Coalition member colleges. Check each college for availability, deadlines, and required materials.

Visit Coalition for College →

University of California (UC) Application

All nine UC undergraduate campuses share one application, filed directly through the UC's own admissions site — not Common App or Coalition.

Visit UC Admissions →

Cal State Apply

The shared application for all 22 California State University campuses, run directly by the CSU system.

Visit Cal State Apply →

ApplyTexas

The shared application accepted by Texas's public universities (and some private ones), run by the State of Texas.

Visit ApplyTexas →

SUNY applySUNY

The applySUNY application lets you apply to multiple participating SUNY campuses. Check the participating-campus list and any campus-specific materials before submitting.

Visit applySUNY →
Don't see your state? Other universities and community colleges may use their own application. Start on the school’s official admissions page and check required materials, deadlines, and fees. Ask about a fee waiver when needed; fees and application time vary by institution.
The process

Step by step, start to submit

1

Build your list

Pick a mix of reach, match and safety schools. For each one, note its application deadline and which system it uses — Common App, Coalition, or its own direct portal.

2

Create your account(s)

Set up an account on whichever system(s) your schools use. If a school runs its own portal (like the UC or SUNY systems above), you'll create a separate account there.

3

Gather your materials

Transcripts, test scores (if a school requires them), your activities list, and basic biographical info. Ask your counselor how transcripts get sent — most high schools send them electronically.

4

Write your essays

Check each college's writing requirements before drafting. Common App colleges can make the personal essay required or optional and may add supplements. UC first-year applicants answer four personal insight questions; most Cal State applicants do not submit essays. Prepare the writing your actual colleges request.

5

Line up recommendation letters early

Ask teachers or your counselor well before the deadline. Give them your resume, your list of schools, and the actual due dates so they're not rushing.

6

File your financial aid forms

Submit the FAFSA (and the CSS Profile if a school requires it) as early as you can — aid is often awarded on a rolling basis, and some state and school deadlines come earlier than you'd expect.

See our Financial Aid Tools →
7

Submit before the deadline

Pay attention to which deadline type each school is using for you (see below) — then submit with time to spare in case of a last-minute technical issue.

8

Track every application after you submit

Each school opens its own applicant status portal once you apply. Check it regularly and respond quickly to any request for more information.

Know your deadline type

Early Decision, Early Action, Regular Decision

Deadlines aren't all the same kind — applying under the wrong one for your situation can cost you flexibility (or an advantage).

Early Decision (binding)

Apply early and you're committing to enroll there if accepted. Only use this for your true first-choice school, since you'll withdraw other applications if admitted.

Early Action (non-binding)

Apply early and hear back sooner, but you're not obligated to enroll. You can usually apply Early Action to multiple schools and still decide later.

Regular Decision

The standard, later deadline most applicants use. No commitment either way, but financial aid and housing priority can sometimes be better for students who apply earlier in the cycle.

Exact deadline dates shift year to year and school to school — always confirm the current date directly on each school's own admissions page rather than assuming it matches last year.
