Student Resource Center

Mental health & basic needs

Support for the things that make it hard to focus on school in the first place — mental health, food, and housing stability. These resources are free, real, and not run by us.

If you're in crisis right now

These are free, confidential, and available 24/7. You don't have to be in danger to call — struggling is enough of a reason.

988
Call or text the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, 24/7
741741
Text HOME to the Crisis Text Line, 24/7
911
For an immediate medical emergency
MENTAL HEALTH

Ongoing support, not just crisis support

Your campus counseling center

Most colleges offer a set number of free counseling sessions per year as part of tuition — usually the fastest way to get connected to care.

Check your school's health services site

SAMHSA National Helpline

Free, confidential, 24/7 referral service for mental health and substance use support. 1-800-662-4357.

Visit SAMHSA →

NAMI (National Alliance on Mental Illness)

Education, support groups, and a helpline for anyone navigating a mental health condition, including students.

Visit NAMI →

National Alliance for Eating Disorders

A free helpline and referral service specifically for eating disorder support.

Visit the Alliance →
FOOD & HOUSING STABILITY

If money is tight for essentials

Your campus basic needs office or food pantry

A large and growing number of colleges run a free food pantry and an emergency aid fund — search "[your school] food pantry" or "basic needs office" to find yours.

Search your school's site

211

A free, nationwide helpline that connects you to local food, housing, utility, and financial assistance programs by ZIP code.

Visit 211.org →

SNAP (food assistance)

Many college students qualify for SNAP food benefits and don't realize it — eligibility rules for students changed in recent years. Check your state's page to see if you qualify.

Check student eligibility →

Feeding America food bank locator

Find a food bank or pantry near you by ZIP code, no enrollment or paperwork required to search.

Find a food bank →
Asking for help here isn't a last resort. Campus basic needs offices exist because this is common — most are set up to help quickly and quietly.
